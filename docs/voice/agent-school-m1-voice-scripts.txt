FILE: l003.wav   |   LESSON 3: AI Can't See the Word "Strawberry" — Tokens Explained
============================================================
AI can't see the word "strawberry." Seriously. Welcome to Agent School — lesson three.
Yesterday: machines learn from examples. Today: what those examples actually look like to the machine.
AI doesn't read letters. It reads tokens — chunks of text. "Strawberry" might become "straw"… and "berry."
Each token becomes a number. So the model sees a short list of numbers — not ten letters.
That's why some models famously miscounted the R's in strawberry. They never saw the letters!
In English, one token is roughly three quarters of a word. A hundred words? About a hundred and thirty tokens.
And tokens matter for you, because AI is priced in tokens, and its memory is measured in tokens.
Remember: AI doesn't read words. It reads tokens.

AI turns every sentence… into coordinates. Welcome to Agent School — lesson ten.

These maps have hundreds or thousands of dimensions. We can't picture them — but the math of distance still works.
Remember: embeddings put meaning on a map. Close points, similar meaning.

FILE: l012.wav   |   LESSON 12: Stop Fine-Tuning — Prompt vs RAG vs Fine-Tune
============================================================
Stop fine-tuning. At least… not yet. Welcome to Agent School — lesson twelve.
You now know three ways to make AI fit your job. Today: which one to pick.
One: prompting. Change the instructions. Free, instant — always try this first.
Two: RAG. Give it your knowledge — documents, policies, products. Perfect when the facts change.
Three: fine-tuning. Retrain the model on your examples. It changes how it behaves — its style, its format — not so much what it knows.
Most people jump straight to fine-tuning… to teach the AI facts. That's the expensive way to do RAG.
So the order is: prompt first. Add RAG when it needs knowledge. Fine-tune only when it needs a new behavior you can't prompt.
Remember: prompt, then RAG, then fine-tune. In that order.

This is the USB-C of AI. Welcome to Agent School — lesson sixteen.
Yesterday: tools let AI act. Today's problem: every app needed its own custom tool code.
MCP — the Model Context Protocol — fixes that. Anthropic released it as an open standard in late 2024.
Remember drawers full of different chargers? USB-C made one plug fit everything. MCP does that for AI tools.
Build an MCP server once — for your calendar, your database, your files — and any AI app that speaks MCP can plug into it.
And it spread fast. OpenAI, Google and many others added support.
A plug that fits everything also needs care: only connect servers you trust, and give them the least access they need.
Remember: build the tool once. Plug it into any AI. That's MCP.

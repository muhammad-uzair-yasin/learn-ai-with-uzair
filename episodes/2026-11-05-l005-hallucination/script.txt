Why does AI lie… with a straight face? Welcome to Agent School — lesson five.
Remember lesson one: AI predicts what's likely, not what's true. Today we see what that costs.
In 2023, lawyers in New York filed a court brief full of cases ChatGPT had cited. The cases didn't exist. The judge fined them five thousand dollars.
That's a hallucination: a confident answer that's made up. The model fills the gap with something that sounds right.
Why so confident? Because sounding sure is just another pattern. Fluent text and true text look the same to a prediction machine.
Three fixes. One: give it the real source to read. Two: ask it to quote where each fact came from. Three: let it say "I don't know."
Remember: fluent is not the same as true.

Nobody programmed ChatGPT's answers. Not one. Welcome back to Agent School — lesson two.
Yesterday we learned AI predicts the next word. Today: how it learned to predict so well.
Old software was rules. A programmer wrote: if this, then that. Every answer, typed by a human.
Machine learning flips it. You don't write the rules. You show examples… and the machine finds the rules itself.
Here's how. Hide the next word in a sentence. The model guesses. It's wrong. A little math nudges it… to be less wrong next time. Then again. And again. Billions of times.
Those nudges tweak billions of tiny numbers called parameters. Nobody sets them by hand. They're learned.
That's why even its creators can't point to the line of code that writes an answer. There isn't one.
Remember: programmers write rules. Machines learn them from examples.
